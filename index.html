Uncolored land = 0 hours; gray = no forecast data. Letters in large blank areas mark the limit that failed most hours (T/RH/W/G/V, P = days since wetting rain): red = too hot/dry/windy/gusty or too long since rain, blue = too cold/moist/calm, mixing height too low, or rain too recent.

Days since wetting rain counts back to the last day with ≥ 0.10 in of precipitation (snow as water; 0 = rain that day). A whole-day value, so it passes or fails every hour of a day alike.

Wind is the model's 10-m wind (roughly 15–20% above 20-ft wind); mixing height is the model boundary-layer height. Saved prescriptions stay in this browser only.

Prescription scenarios: Worsnop et al. 2026, Fire Ecology 22:51. Rain: gridMET. Citations ↓

Citations and data sources

Prescription scenarios and the days-since-wetting-rain limit are built on the work of Rochelle Worsnop and co-authors, who paired 15,468 Northern California burn permits (PFIRS, 2011–2024) with the weather on each burn day and published the range of conditions practitioners actually burned under, by month, burn type, vegetation and elevation. The Timber, Grass/Shrub and Pile presets draw on their burn-day criteria, with input from California burners' own burn plan prescriptions.

  • Worsnop, R.P., A. Hoell, B.J. Hatchett, T.B. Chapman, M.L. Breeden, Z. Tolby, K.C. Short and M. Hobbins. 2026. Characterizing windows of opportunity for prescribed pile and broadcast burning in Northern California. Fire Ecology 22:51. https://doi.org/10.1186/s42408-026-00492-6
  • Holden, Z.A., A. Swanson, C.H. Luce, W.M. Jolly, M. Maneta, J.W. Oyler, D.A. Warren, R. Parsons and D. Affleck. 2018. Decreasing fire season precipitation increased recent western US forest wildfire activity. PNAS 115(36):E8349–E8357 — the 0.10 in (2.54 mm) wetting-rain threshold, as used by Worsnop et al.
  • Observed precipitation: gridMET (daily, 4 km). Abatzoglou, J.T. 2013. Development of gridded surface meteorological data for ecological applications and modelling. International Journal of Climatology 33:121–131. https://doi.org/10.1002/joc.3413
  • Forecast weather: Open-Meteo forecast API (NOAA GFS/NBM/HRRR blend), CC-BY-4.0.
  • Boundaries and cities: Natural Earth (public domain).
